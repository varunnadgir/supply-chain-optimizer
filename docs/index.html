Design a fulfillment network for Brazil

Olist, a Brazilian online marketplace, ships every order straight from the seller, and 72% of items leave from São Paulo state. Change the assumptions on the left and a mixed-integer optimization model chooses where to build fulfillment centers, how big to make them and which customers each one serves. It solves live in your browser, using 84,523 real shipments from September 2017 to August 2018.

What the model does

  • Every item either ships direct from its seller, as today, or goes by truck to a center and then by parcel to the customer.
  • The model picks sites and building sizes from 60 candidate cities to minimize building costs plus freight (plus CO2 at the carbon price), under the limits you set.
  • Freight comes from a rate card fitted to real parcel prices. Building costs, sizes and emission factors are assumptions.
  • This page plans for one future. The notebook on uncertainty plans for 50 at once.
Data: Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Solver: HiGHS, compiled to WebAssembly by highs-js (MIT). Amounts in Brazilian reais (R$). The dataset is a sample of Olist's orders, so the percentages matter more than the totals.
